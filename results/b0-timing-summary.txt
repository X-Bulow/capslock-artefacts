B0 A3 timing summary (five-way, AC-powered)
================================================

Measurement conditions
----------------------
Timing host: x86-64 Windows/WSL2 laptop, connected to AC power
Preparation: Windows restarted before the run; no Isabelle workload running
Pre-run WSL state: 8.0 GiB available memory, 0 B swap used, load average
0.22 / 0.27 / 0.14, Docker ready
Workload: 1000 timed miniz_oxide decompression iterations per measurement
Input: deterministic 4096-byte buffer, compressed to 338 bytes
Repetitions: 5 interleaved trials per variant
Statistic: arithmetic mean of the 5 measurements
Correctness: all 25 observations produced checksum 2554592075418854092

Timing boundaries
-----------------
elapsed_ns is measured by Instant around only the decompression loop. It
excludes Docker/process startup, QEMU initialisation, input construction,
compression and one untimed warm-up decompression.

wall_ns surrounds the complete docker run. It includes those fixed startup
and shutdown costs as well as the timed workload. Internal elapsed time is
therefore used for the workload ratios below; both measurements are retained.

Mean end-to-end wall times
--------------------------
variant                         mean seconds
native x86-64, optimised          0.819404348
native x86-64, unoptimised        0.792514341
stock qemu, unoptimised RISC-V    1.944428335
CapsLock QEMU, checking no-op     9.735284999
full CapsLock QEMU              183.782600379

For the very short native measurements, roughly 0.7 to 0.8 seconds of fixed
Docker/process work dominates wall_ns. The small reversal between the two
native wall means is startup noise and does not mean the unoptimised workload
is faster. The internal timer measures the expected 19.464x optimisation gap.

Incremental ratios
------------------
unoptimised native / optimised native: 19.463558x
stock QEMU / unoptimised native:        13.350831x
CapsLock no-op / stock QEMU:             7.540235x
full CapsLock / CapsLock no-op:          20.681664x

The stock/unoptimised-native comparison isolates cross-ISA emulation more
cleanly than stock/optimised-native. The latter also includes the 19.464x
effect of compiling the guest without optimisation.

Absolute-slowdown decomposition
-------------------------------
full - optimised native: 181.937301437 s
full - CapsLock no-op:   173.144540392 s

(full - no-op) / (full - optimised native): 95.167148%
(no-op - optimised native) / (full - optimised native): 4.832852%

The first percentage is the portion of the full-over-optimised-native elapsed
increase added when complete capability checking is enabled over the no-op
CapsLock configuration. The complement includes ordinary emulation, the
unoptimised and instrumented guest, and CapsLock-QEMU overhead, so it is not a
pure cross-ISA emulation percentage.

Run-to-run stability
--------------------
variant                         coefficient of variation
native x86-64, optimised                         4.869%
native x86-64, unoptimised                       6.148%
stock qemu, unoptimised RISC-V                   8.855%
CapsLock QEMU, checking no-op                    4.724%
full CapsLock QEMU                               2.524%

The AC-powered run was substantially faster than the earlier battery-powered
run: 46.5% for optimised native, 42.9% for unoptimised native, 34.8% for stock
QEMU, 38.4% for CapsLock no-op and 41.2% for full CapsLock. This supports using
the AC-powered observations as the controlled result and retaining the battery
run only as evidence of host power-state sensitivity.
